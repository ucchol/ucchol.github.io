G in iD

I’m a PhD student in Computer Science at The University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP), advised by Christopher D. Kiekintveld. My research brings together game theory, cognitive science, and cybersecurity to study how people make decisions during cyber attacks.

I work on cyber deception and attacker decision-making. Through behavioral experiments, I examine how the number of choices and the amount of information affect an attacker’s decisions. I also develop game-theoretic models to study how defenders should plan when their assumptions about an attacker may be wrong.

Alongside this work, I study methods for detecting malware and malicious domains, with an emphasis on threats that were not seen during training. I collaborate with researchers at UTEP and the DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory. You can read more about these projects and our publications on the research page.

About me. I received my MS in Computer Science from UTEP in 2023 and my BSc in Electronics and Communication Engineering from Khulna University of Engineering and Technology, Bangladesh, in 2016. Before graduate school, I worked in service operations at bKash and as a system administrator at REVE Systems. At UTEP, I have served as a teaching assistant for Operating Systems, Computer Networks, and Machine Learning. I have also been assigned as a lab instructor for Introduction to Computational Thinking (C and Java).
