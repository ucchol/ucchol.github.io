G in iD

I’m a PhD student in Computer Science at The University of Texas at El Paso, advised by Christopher D. Kiekintveld. My research asks how we can defend systems when our understanding of the attacker is incomplete. I use game theory to design defenses under uncertainty, cognitive science to study how people make decisions during cyber tasks, and machine learning to evaluate whether detectors recognize unfamiliar threats. I built a game-theoretic solver and a cyber–physical testbed for studying decoy placement and designed human-subject experiments on how people make choices in cyber tasks.

As a graduate teaching assistant at UTEP, I have supported courses in operating systems, computer networks, and machine learning. I have also led labs in introductory computational thinking using C and Java. I served on the program committee for the AI for Social Impact track at AAAI 2026 and was selected to serve again for AAAI 2027. I have reviewed papers for AAMAS, ICNC, and ICECER.

I earned my MS in Computer Science from The University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP) in 2023 and my BSc in Electronics and Communication Engineering from Khulna University of Engineering and Technology (KUET), Bangladesh in 2016. Before graduate school, I worked in service operations at bKash and as a systems administrator at REVE Systems in Bangladesh. Working with production infrastructure gave me a practical perspective on security decisions and their consequences for the people and services that depend on those systems.

I also venture into hands-on projects to sharpen my engineering skills and keep pace with advances in AI. I build and containerize detection services, develop novelty scoring and DNS log replay tools, and create simulations that let me compare reinforcement learning, optimization, and short-horizon planning under limited resources and imperfect information.
